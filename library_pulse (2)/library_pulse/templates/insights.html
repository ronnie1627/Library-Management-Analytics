What the Data Shows

A plain-language reading of the patterns behind the charts — the "so what" for each panel.

Sci-Fi and Technology are pulling ahead

Both genres show a steady upward slope across the two-year window while most other genres stay flat, a signal worth acting on for next quarter's acquisitions budget.

Exam months double the load

March–April and October–November see roughly 1.8× the checkout volume of a typical month, while June–July and December are quiet. Staffing and hold-shelf capacity could flex around this rhythm instead of staying flat year-round.

A small group drives most of the overdue problem

The "Fine-Prone" segment is a minority of members but accounts for a disproportionate share of late returns and fines — a short reminder-email campaign targeted at just this group would likely move the overdue rate more than a blanket policy change.

Central Library carries the branch load

One branch handles a visibly larger share of circulation than the other three combined, which is useful context if the next step is deciding where to expand hours or add copies.

Afternoons on weekdays are peak hours

The day/hour heatmap shows a clear concentration in weekday afternoons, with weekends and early mornings consistently quiet — useful for scheduling staff and planning maintenance windows.
